## Field note: v1 with partial signals shows REGRESSION (under-coverage caveat)

Tested the v1 pipeline with 7 RBs coded for 2022 (extractor stopped early at credit-balance limit). Result:

- v0 nosignals 2022: Spearman 0.3901
- v1 signals 2022 (7 of 10 RBs coded, plus 0 of 100 non-RBs): Spearman 0.3778

v1 is consistently SLIGHTLY WORSE than v0 across both 4-RB and 7-RB partial samples (0.376 → 0.378 → vs 0.390). The signal-driven boost to bellcow RBs is hurting correlation on the 3-year cumulative horizon. Possible interpretations:

1. **Boost magnitude miscalibrated for 3-year horizon.** A +8 RB-score boost for "bellcow" might be right for next-season but too aggressive when scoring against 3-year cumulative (where injury risk + RB cliff dominate).
2. **Coded sample is bellcow-heavy.** 6 of 7 coded RBs are bellcow / lead_back / committee. Bellcow boost moves them up; their 3-year actual rankings often disappoint (Najee declined; CMC injured; Ekeler aged out; Etienne had OL changes).
3. **Hard-gate path may not be firing.** Javonte was coded as committee_member but the gate didn't visibly cap his score at 60. Worth investigating in `src/lib/engine/evaluation/rubrics/rb.ts`.

This is real calibration intelligence. The v0 signal-free baseline beating v1 with partial bellcow signals is exactly the kind of finding the calibration sprint is supposed to produce. Possible product responses:

- Reduce the bellcow boost from +8 to something smaller (e.g., +3-4)
- Apply the boost only on next-season scoring; gate it down for 3-year horizon
- Add a "bellcow durability prior" using age + carries-per-year

Hold v1 numbers as preliminary. Do NOT publish v1 numbers until either full coverage lands or boost calibration is updated.
